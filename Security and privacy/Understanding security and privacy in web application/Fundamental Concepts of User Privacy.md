# Fundamental Concepts of User Privacy

## Overview

**User privacy** is your right to control **how your personal data is collected, used, and shared online**. Modern privacy frameworks rely on several core building blocks to protect your digital footprint.

| # | Concept | In One Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personally Identifiable Information (PII) | Data that can identify you |
| 2 | Confidentiality | Keeping your data from unauthorized access |
| 3 | Data minimization | Collect only what is needed |
| 4 | Purpose limitation | Use data only for the stated reason |
| 5 | Tracking | Monitoring your movements across the internet |
| 6 | Consent | You must actively opt in |
| 7 | Right to be forgotten | You can demand deletion of your data |

---

## 1. Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

PII is **any data that can directly or indirectly pinpoint your identity**.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Direct identifiers** | Full name, home address, national identification number |
| **Indirect identifiers** | IP address, device serial number, precise location history |

---

## 2. Confidentiality

Companies must keep PII safe from unauthorized access using:

- **Strong encryption**
- **Access controls**

> **When it breaks:** If an unauthorized third party reads your records or leaks your PII, your confidentiality has been broken.

---

## 3. Data Minimization

Companies should collect **only the absolute minimum amount of PII** needed to complete a specific task.

**Example:** A newsletter signup needs your email address. It does not need your home address or birth date.

---

## 4. Purpose Limitation

Once a business collects your data for a specific reason, it **cannot legally use it for anything else** later.

**Example:** You give your contact info to receive order updates. The company cannot then sell it to advertising networks.

> **How 3 and 4 work together:** Minimization limits **how much** data is collected. Purpose limitation limits **what it can be used for**.

---

## 5. Tracking

Tracking is the **continuous monitoring of your online movements across the internet**. The principles above directly combat it.

**Common methods used by advertising networks:**

- Scripts
- Device fingerprinting
- Browser history logs

These are used to trace your behavior and **compile detailed profiles without your knowledge**.

---

## 6. User Consent

Consent is the **bedrock of individual control**. True privacy requires consent that is:

- **Informed:** you understand what is being collected and why
- **Explicit:** you give a clear, deliberate approval
- **Opt-in:** you actively choose to allow tracking, rather than being forced into it

---

## 7. Right to Be Forgotten (Data Erasure)

Privacy laws give you the **legal right to request that an organization permanently delete** all your personal records and tracking history from its databases, for example once you close your account.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **PII** includes both direct identifiers (name, address) and indirect ones (IP address, location history).
- **Confidentiality** protects PII through encryption and access controls.
- **Data minimization** limits how much is collected. **Purpose limitation** limits how it is used.
- **Tracking** builds hidden profiles, so **informed, explicit, opt-in consent** is essential.
- The **right to be forgotten** lets you demand permanent deletion of your data.
- The next lecture covers the **privacy laws and the bodies responsible** for enforcing them.
